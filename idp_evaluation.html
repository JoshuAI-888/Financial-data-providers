Investment Data Platform (IDP) Evaluation

A requirements-and-evaluation framework for selecting an Investment Data Platform for an institutional multi-asset manager — public equity, fixed income, credit, ETFs, FX and private equity, across the US, UK, Australia, New Zealand and Asia — where Databricks is the strategic enterprise data & AI platform. The governing principle: buy investment-domain complexity; build differentiated investment intelligence. This page renders the framework — 12 weighted domains, 22 hard gates, the scoring methodology, a 10-vendor market screen and the proof-of-value design — as interactive tools.

Executive purpose

The purpose of an Investment Data Platform is not to replace Databricks with another generic enterprise data platform. It is to provide specialised investment-domain capabilities that would be costly, slow, risky or non-differentiating to build internally — mastering, validation, reconciliation, corporate actions, IBOR, point-in-time history, private-market structures — while the organisation retains its differentiated analytics, research models, data products and AI intellectual property inside Databricks.

Buy investment-domain complexity; build differentiated investment intelligence. The selected IDP should master, validate, reconcile, contextualise and operationalise investment data while exposing it through open, governed, performant interfaces to Databricks, analytical tools, investment users and enterprise AI agents.
How to read the vendor content on this page. The 10-vendor assessment is a Stage-1 paper screen compiled from public documentation and press — it is provisional and evidence-capped (documented capability scores no higher than 3–4; a real score of 4–5 requires a named production reference or the organisation's own proof-of-value). It is a starting point for an RFI, not a recommendation or a substitute for the PoV.

Strategic outcomes the platform is judged against

Judged on measurable business outcomes, not feature quantity:

  • Reduce manual price-validation effort by ≥ 80%.
  • Establish a governed enterprise security master.
  • Validated portfolio data available by 07:00 local business time.
  • Onboard ≥ 10 strategically important datasets.
  • Analysts self-serve governed data.
  • AI research agents consume governed data with provenance, entitlements & auditability.
  • Daily total-portfolio exposure across public and private.
  • Reliable point-in-time reconstruction for ≥ 5 years.
  • Better exception detection & resolution without outsourcing control.
  • An extensible foundation for new asset classes, datasets, models and AI — no repeat integration projects.

Target architecture

The IDP masters investment-domain data and exposes it through open, governed interfaces; Databricks holds enterprise analytics, research data products, quant models and AI. The IDP should strengthen Databricks, not create a second analytical estate.

Delivery roadmap

The strategic requirement is far larger than a credible 30–60 day build, so the evaluation separates immediate proof of value from long-term platform capability.

Phase 0 · 30–60 days

Evaluation & proof of value

Prove four things on real data: (A) security & price master across equities/ETF/govvies/ credit/FX; (B) price validation with ≥ 80% less manual effort; (C) mastered data live in the evaluation Databricks; (D) a governed AI use case preserving permissions, provenance, auditability. Slides and canned demos do not count.

Phase 1 · 3–6 months

Production foundation

10 production datasets · 07:00 portfolio SLA · security/entity/price master · benchmarks · transaction ingestion · intraday positions · reconciliation · corporate actions · 5 years history & point-in-time · initial IBOR · total-portfolio foundation · analyst self-service · governed Databricks publication.

Phase 2 · 6–18 months

Strategic scale

Private-market document ingestion & GP extraction · look-through exposure · private cash flows · performance & attribution · advanced risk analytics · anomaly detection · research & operational agents · controlled write-back · cross-domain data products.

Evaluation principles

Evidence over claims

A feature is not supported because a salesperson, a roadmap, a marketing page or a partner says so. Scores are capped by the strength of evidence supplied.

Production over demonstration

Distinguish GA from private/public preview, beta, early-adopter and roadmap. An early-adopter AI programme can be interesting yet still score below a proven production capability.

Open architecture over capture

The winner should increase the strategic value of Databricks — not make it redundant. Wholesale movement of analytics into a proprietary cloud is penalised.

Outcome over feature count · control over outsourcing

Fewer features can outrank more if automation, data quality, auditability, TCO and openness are better — and managed services must augment internal control, not hide the business rules.

Proof-of-value test design

Every finalist completes the same test on the same data under the same rules — to stop any vendor controlling the evaluation with a polished canned demonstration. Provide ~500–1,000 instruments across equities, ETFs, govvies, credit, FX and private holdings, seeded with deliberately difficult conditions: duplicate securities, multiple listings, changed tickers, stale/outlier/missing prices, conflicting sources, bad identifiers, a corporate action, a historical correction, incomplete private data, an unfamiliar private-market PDF, and transaction/position records.

Test 1 · Instrument & entity mastering

Measure auto-match rate, false-match rate, unresolved rate, human touches per 1,000, elapsed time. False matches penalised harder than unresolved records.

Test 2 · Price validation

Baseline human review effort vs PoV effort. Target ≥ 80% reduction in manual price-validation effort without reducing agreed controls.

Test 3 · 07:00 portfolio SLA

Simulate an overnight cycle; measure feed completeness, outstanding exceptions, portfolio completeness, timeliness, operational visibility.

Test 4 · Databricks

Real mastered data becomes queryable in the evaluation Databricks: SQL, Python, Unity Catalog, schema, lineage, refresh, entitlements.

Test 5 · Unseen dataset

Introduce a previously unseen dataset mid-PoV; measure elapsed hours to ingested → mapped → validated → documented → governed → queryable.

Test 6 · Point-in-time

"What would a user have seen at timestamp X, before correction Y arrived?" — proof required from stored events/versions, not a destructive overwrite.

Test 7 · Private-equity document

Extract fund, company, commitment, capital call, distribution, NAV, valuation, currency, date from an unfamiliar GP document — each field linked to evidence.

Test 8 · Enterprise AI model

Using the organisation's chosen LLM: "What material portfolio exposures changed today, what caused them, and what source evidence supports the answer?" — vendor must not require its proprietary model.

Test 9 · User self-service

Representatives from PM, analysis, operations and quant complete defined tasks without vendor assistance.

Test 10 · Exit

Export raw + mastered data, historical versions, schemas, mappings, rules, exceptions, overrides, audit records, metadata. No realistic exit → major reversibility penalty.

Red flags to investigate

  • "Yes, we support that" without evidence.
  • IBOR based primarily on overnight snapshots.
  • Point-in-time achieved only by exporting to an external warehouse.
  • AI capability requiring the vendor's proprietary LLM.
  • AI roadmap presented as GA capability.
  • MCP server exposing only a small demonstration subset.
  • "Databricks integration" that is actually scheduled CSV export.
  • Lineage that stops at the vendor's ingestion boundary.
  • No separation of content licence and platform fee.
  • AUM pricing with steep nonlinear growth.
  • Every new dataset requires professional services.
  • Model extensions require vendor coding.
  • Inability to export configuration.
  • Contract gives the vendor rights over derived client data.
  • Manual exception work hidden inside "managed service".
  • Unresolved APAC support model.
  • Many acquired components with inconsistent data models.
  • Roadmap dependent on unreleased platform consolidation.
  • References far larger than this org, with huge implementation teams.
  • An implementation proposal that cannot define a 30–60 day PoV, or no meaningful answer on AI licensing rights.
