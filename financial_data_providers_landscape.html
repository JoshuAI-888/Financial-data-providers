Executive summary

The institutional data market is not one contest but several, each with its own incumbent and its own moat. No single vendor leads every asset class; the "winners" won by owning a specific proprietary asset — an evaluated-pricing book, an index franchise, a ratings history, a private-capital research operation, or a two-sided network — that competitors cannot cheaply replicate. The most durable moats are network effects (Bloomberg messaging, eVestment's manager-allocator network, MSCI benchmarks) and regulatory/embedded workflows (ratings, evaluated prices used for fund NAV).

Cross-asset terminals

Bloomberg leads on fixed income, news and its messaging network; LSEG is the credible #2 (FTSE Russell, WM/R FX, World-Check, open delivery); FactSet wins buy-side analytics, support and open APIs at ~half Bloomberg's cost.

Fundamentals, ratings & credit

S&P owns fundamentals breadth (Compustat) + ratings; Moody's owns credit risk (ratings + Orbis + EDF/RiskCalc); Morningstar owns managed-investments, ESG (Sustainalytics) and DBRS credit.

Private markets

PitchBook leads private deal breadth; Preqin owns LP–GP & fund performance; CB Insights owns predictive venture signals; With Intelligence owns allocator + hedge-fund data.

Indexes, risk & FI infrastructure

MSCI owns global/EM equity indexes + factor risk (Barra) + ESG; ICE owns fixed-income evaluated pricing + credit indices; Nasdaq/eVestment owns the institutional manager-selection network.

API-native challengers

FMP, Polygon, Intrinio, Finnhub, Xignite invert the model: self-serve, public pricing, clean REST/WebSocket. They win on cost, speed and developer experience — not breadth, private markets, or evaluated FI pricing.

The structural takeaway

Consolidation is intensifying: BlackRock→Preqin, S&P→With Intelligence, MSCI→Burgiss, Morningstar→PitchBook. Buyers increasingly assemble a portfolio of specialists rather than a single terminal.

Why the winners won — five recurring edge patterns

  • Proprietary evaluated / computed data. Bloomberg BVAL and ICE evaluated pricing price millions of illiquid bonds daily — audit-defensible numbers embedded in fund NAV and back-office workflow, so switching cost is systemic.
  • Network effects. Bloomberg's messaging network, eVestment's two-sided manager↔allocator database, and MSCI's benchmark gravity (~US$18.3T benchmarked) each get more valuable as more participants join — the hardest moat to attack on price alone.
  • A research operation at scale. PitchBook's large human-research team and Preqin's 20+ years of FOIA/self-reported fund performance are expensive, slow-to-build assets that pure-tech entrants cannot shortcut.
  • Franchise histories. Compustat point-in-time fundamentals (since 1987), Moody's/S&P ratings histories, and ICE BofA index history are decades deep — the quant and credit "gold standards."
  • Regulatory embedding. Ratings and evaluated prices are woven into regulation, mandates and NAV processes; demand is structurally sticky regardless of UX.

Where the runners-up fall short

The second- and third-place providers in each arena are rarely "worse" — they lose a specific dimension. LSEG and FactSet trail Bloomberg on fixed-income depth and the messaging network, not on data quality. Preqin trails PitchBook on deal/company granularity but leads it on LP-side fund performance. CB Insights trails both on transactional depth but leads on predictive signals. The lesson: rank is asset-class- and use-case-specific, which is exactly why this report uses a capability map rather than a single league table.

Asset-class views — who leads and why

The same providers rank differently depending on the asset class. Below, for each class: the leader and its edge, then the notable runners-up and what keeps them off top.

① Public equity

Leader — Bloomberg, on real-time cross-asset breadth, news and the integrated trading/messaging stack; S&P (Capital IQ / Compustat) and FactSet are effectively co-leaders on the data itself.

Why they lead
  • Bloomberg — decades of proprietary pricing, the on-terminal counterparty network, and depth no one matches on live markets + news.
  • S&P — Compustat point-in-time fundamentals (since 1987) are the quant gold standard; +150k public companies, estimates, filings, transcripts.
  • FactSet — best-in-class buy-side portfolio analytics, symbology concordance and support, at materially lower cost.
Runners-up & what holds them back
  • LSEG — deep global equities + I/B/E/S + StarMine, but workflow depth and the missing messaging network keep it #2 to Bloomberg.
  • Morningstar — strong through a funds/independent-research lens, weaker on deep as-reported fundamentals and real-time.
  • MSCI — leads equity indexes/factor risk, not single-name fundamentals or a terminal.

② Private equity & venture

Leader — PitchBook for private deal/company/investor breadth; Preqin leads the distinct LP–GP & fund-performance sub-domain.

Why they lead
  • PitchBook — ~4.5m companies, 525k+ investors, 125k+ funds; a large human-research operation + relationship graph, plus LCD leveraged-loan/credit via Morningstar.
  • Preqin — 20+ years of FOIA/self-reported fund performance (IRR/TVPI/DPI) and LP coverage — the LP-side default, now backed by BlackRock/Aladdin.
Runners-up & what holds them back
  • CB Insights — best on predictive venture/emerging-tech signals (Mosaic), but thinner transactional deal/fund depth; no LP-GP or credit.
  • With Intelligence — owns allocator + hedge-fund data (Eurekahedge/HFM); less deal/LP-performance depth.
  • S&P / MSCI-Burgiss — strong private-company reference and benchmarking respectively, but not deal-sourcing leaders.

③ Fixed income & credit

Leaders — Bloomberg & ICE (evaluated pricing / indices); Moody's and S&P lead credit ratings & risk.

Why they lead
  • Bloomberg — BVAL evaluated pricing + Bloomberg fixed-income indices; the practitioner default for live FI and reference.
  • ICE — ~2.7m securities priced daily (munis/structured/loans depth), ICE BofA credit indices, SFTI/Consolidated Feed — embedded in fund-admin/NAV.
  • Moody's / S&P — the ratings duopoly plus quantitative credit (EDF/RiskCalc) and issuer/issue histories.
Runners-up & what holds them back
  • LSEG — broad FI reference/pricing, but evaluated-pricing depth trails Bloomberg/ICE.
  • FactSet — solid FI analytics; often relies on partners for evaluated pricing.
  • PitchBook (LCD) — leads leveraged-loan / private credit news+data specifically, not broad public FI.

④ Other / cross-asset (indexes · ratings · ESG · risk · allocator & alt-data)

Here leadership fragments by sub-domain — each winner owns a franchise asset.

Sub-domain leaders & why
  • Equity indexes & factor risk — MSCI (benchmark network effect + Barra incumbency); FTSE Russell (LSEG) and S&P DJI are the main rivals.
  • ESG data — MSCI & Sustainalytics (Morningstar) lead on coverage, though ESG-ratings methodology credibility is contested.
  • Credit ratings — Moody's & S&P (regulatory-embedded duopoly); DBRS (Morningstar) is the strong #4, leading in Canada.
  • Manager / allocator intelligence — Nasdaq eVestment (two-sided network); With Intelligence owns direct-from-allocator + hedge-fund data.
  • Predictive alt-data signals — CB Insights (Mosaic, patents/hiring/web signals).
  • Macro / academic time-series — LSEG Datastream (~120 years of history).
Structural notes
  • Franchise assets (FTSE Russell, WM/R FX fixing, World-Check, ICE BofA, Compustat) are hard to dislodge because they are embedded in benchmarks, regulation or workflow.
  • ESG is the most contested arena — low cross-vendor rating correlation and regulatory scrutiny mean "leadership" here is about coverage, not consensus quality.
  • Cost/lock-in backlash is the recurring opening for challengers (Solactive vs. index majors; API-native vendors vs. terminals).

API-native fintech challengers

This tier — FMP, Polygon.io, Intrinio, Finnhub, Xignite — inverts the incumbent playbook: public pricing, self-serve sign-up, a working API key in minutes, and clean JSON/CSV over REST/WebSocket aimed at engineers rather than analysts. The trade-off is deliberate — coverage is US-centric and thinner, fundamentals history shallower, support is community/ticket-based, and private markets + evaluated FI pricing are absent. They win on cost, developer experience and speed-to-integration. Consolidation is arriving here too: Polygon now bills under Massive.com, and Xignite was acquired by QUODD (2023).

Methodology, scope & limitations

Scope

  • Independent, vendor-neutral research. Providers are grouped into five clusters: cross-asset terminals; fundamentals & ratings; private markets; indexes/risk/FI infrastructure; and API-native challengers.
  • Generic framing across the asset classes an institutional multi-asset manager engages in and expands toward — public equity, private markets, fixed income and cross-asset — with no organisation-specific application.
  • 13 core providers profiled in full; 5 API-native vendors covered as a comparative tier.

How the research was done

  • Each provider was researched from public sources: vendor product/API/developer documentation and press for technical facts; independent review platforms (G2, TrustRadius, Gartner Peer Insights, Capterra) and analyst/practitioner commentary for sentiment.
  • Triangulation: customer feedback was corroborated across ≥2 sources because single review pages (notably G2) were frequently blocked or gated from the research environment.
  • Every provider fact-sheet carries its own dated source list (see the accompanying provider-landscape/research/*.md files, which are the full audit trail behind this report).

The capability map is qualitative

The five-level scale (Leader → Minimal) is a directional, relative assessment within the institutional landscape — not a numeric score, weighted model, or ranking. It reflects the analyst reading of each fact-sheet and is intended to orient, not to adjudicate a purchase.

Limitations

  • Pricing is approximate. None of the enterprise vendors publish list prices; figures are third-party estimates flagged "approx." and can vary widely by seats, modules, datasets and delivery.
  • Review scores are indicative. Several review pages were inaccessible; scores were triangulated from secondary summaries.
  • Point-in-time. Ownership, products and figures are as of 10 Aug 2026 and this is a fast-moving, consolidating market (e.g. BlackRock–Preqin, S&P–With Intelligence, MSCI–Burgiss).
  • Not advice. Analytical/educational use only; verify any figure against primary sources before relying on it.

Glossary of key terms

Evaluated pricing — model-plus-analyst valuations for securities that don't trade continuously (esp. bonds); used for fund NAV.

BVAL / CEP — Bloomberg's and ICE's evaluated-pricing services.

Point-in-time (PIT) — data as it was known on a past date (no restatement/survivorship bias); the quant-research standard (e.g. Compustat PIT since 1987).

Factor / risk model (Barra) — decomposes portfolio risk/return into common factors (value, size, momentum, etc.).

LP / GP — Limited Partners (investors, e.g. pensions) and General Partners (fund managers) in private funds.

IRR / TVPI / DPI / PME — private-fund performance metrics (return rate; total value; distributions; public-market equivalent).

Symbology / concordance — mapping the same security/entity across identifier systems (CUSIP, SEDOL, ISIN, FIGI, PermID).

Redistribution / entitlement — licensing controls on who may access or re-share data; the core commercial constraint.

Consolidated / B-PIPE feed — managed real-time market-data streams delivered to enterprise systems.

Warehouse-native delivery — data shared directly into a client's cloud data warehouse (Snowflake / Databricks / AWS) rather than a terminal.
