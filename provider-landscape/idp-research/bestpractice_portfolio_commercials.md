# Best Practice: Total Portfolio, Commercials & Vendor Selection

Principle-level, vendor-neutral. Access date: 2026-08-15.

## 1. Total-portfolio / whole-portfolio view (public + private)
**Best practice:** Maintain one integrated view spanning listed and private assets — knowing what you own, its current value, and where risk sits — with look-through from investment options to underlying holdings. For private markets, this means ingesting GP/manager documents (PDFs, portal extracts, capital-call and distribution notices), normalising them to common identifiers and taxonomies, and modelling cash flows so illiquid assets sit on the same footing as public ones.
**Why it matters:** Total-fund risk, liquidity and exposure can only be assessed if public and private assets are combined; private data is scattered across portals, emails, PDFs and spreadsheets with weak standard identifiers, so it must be actively synthesised.
**If neglected:** Blind spots in concentration, factor and liquidity risk; a fragmented picture that undermines allocation decisions and the credibility of a Total Portfolio Approach.

## 2. Time-to-value / phased implementation
**Best practice:** Prove value before a full build — run a scoped Proof of Value (PoV) with measurable business outcomes, deliver core capabilities first, use pre-built connectors and managed services to shorten time-to-value, and keep manual processes in parallel until the platform is trusted. For ANZ/APAC, confirm in-region support coverage and time-zone alignment.
**Why it matters:** A PoV tests real outcomes (not just feasibility) and surfaces true TCO early; phased delivery with quick wins sustains sponsorship and reduces delivery risk.
**If neglected:** Long, high-risk big-bang programmes; slipped timelines, partial adoption, and value that arrives too late to justify the spend.

## 3. Commercials, TCO, licensing/AI rights, exit & portability
**Best practice:** Evaluate whole-life TCO (licence, implementation, support, scaling), not headline price. Secure explicit rights to own and export all your data, metadata, lineage and derived outputs in standard formats, with no punitive egress/termination fees; clarify data-licensing scope and AI-usage/training rights; define exit criteria and deconversion clauses up front.
**Why it matters:** Lock-in via proprietary formats, hidden fees and forced upgrades makes exit costly or infeasible; portability planning avoids those hidden costs and is increasingly expected for operational-resilience and third-party-risk reasons.
**If neglected:** Escalating fees, technical entrenchment, and difficult, expensive exits that erode negotiating leverage.

## 4. Vendor viability, roadmap credibility & references
**Best practice:** Weigh present-tense, in-production capability over roadmap promises (roadmap ≠ GA). Insist on live configurations, pilots with success metrics, and reference calls with clients at your scale and complexity. Assess financial stability, operational resilience and congruence between marketing claims and operational reality.
**Why it matters:** Proposals state intent; references and production evidence reveal what was actually delivered. Accepting assurances over demonstrated capability leads to missed timelines and partial adoption.
**If neglected:** Betting on undelivered features, buyer's regret, and a vendor that cannot support the workload at scale.
**Sources:** https://complexdiscovery.com/buyers-guide/making-the-subjective-objective-a-scoring-framework-for-evaluating-ediscovery-vendor-viability-in-2026/ ; https://technologymatch.com/blog/10-best-practices-for-a-better-vendor-selection-process (2026-08-15)

## 5. Software-selection method: gates, scoring & evidence discipline
**Best practice:** Use two stages — hard knock-out gates for non-negotiable must-haves (eliminate early), then weighted scoring of survivors against criteria set *before* contacting vendors, on a tight 1–5 scale. Track evidence-confidence: score on verified evidence, and structure PoV testing against your real requirements rather than vendor showcases.
**Why it matters:** Defining criteria after seeing proposals introduces bias and lets demos become feature showcases; a traceable weighted scorecard plus mandatory gates yields defensible, auditable decisions.
**If neglected:** Decisions swayed by the last/slickest demo, non-negotiables traded away against a high aggregate score, and choices no one can justify later.
**Sources:** https://www.businessanalystlearnings.com/ba-techniques/2014/2/27/weighted-scoring-model-a-technique-for-comparing-software-tools ; https://dectrack.com/en/blog/weighted-scoring-model (2026-08-15)
